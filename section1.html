Transmission or Transformation

About 3 minutes

Start here

Think of a climate lesson from your own time at school. What do you remember: the facts, or something you did?

Hold your answer in mind as you read about two ways to teach.

Two ways to teach

Freire described two kinds of education. In banking education, the teacher decides the content alone and “deposits” it in students, who store and repeat it. In problem-posing education, the content starts from students’ own situation and from what they want to know more about.

  • Banking (transmission)The teacher chooses the topic and explains it. Students take notes and show they remember them on a quiz.
  • Problem-posing (transformation)Students bring questions about their own place. Teacher and students investigate them together as co-investigators.

A lot of climate teaching works like banking. The facts are correct, but students only receive them.

Why this matters for climate teaching

Transmission aims at what students can recall. Transformation aims at how students understand their world and what they do in it.

For Freire, the content of a lesson is “neither a gift nor an imposition”. It is the organized “re-presentation” of the things students want to know more about (p. 25).

Salinas et al. also cite the science educator Jay Lemke, who argues that school science has become cut off from students’ daily lives and from the social and moral questions older students care about (p. 27). Climate change is a daily-life issue, so teaching it as facts to copy hides the part that matters most to students.

In the reading · p. 35

Nataly Bastiás, an elementary teacher in Chile and one of the article’s authors, planned a unit on local wetlands with two colleagues. The teachers knew nothing about the local wetlands when they started. They learned alongside their students, and invited a wetlands specialist and a local activist as guest speakers. Teachers and students investigated the topic together.

Source: Salinas et al. (2023), pp. 24–27, quoting Freire, Pedagogy of the Oppressed.
